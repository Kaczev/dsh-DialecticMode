# Verify: deciding what to keep, what to change, and how far to look

Four moves for the moment after a draft exists. They decide whether to revise at all, how much of the
existing thing survives, and where the work stops. The persona carries the gate and the two-round
limit; this file is the set of moves available inside that limit.

## 1. Do not revise unless you can name the specific defect — then change only what it requires

Determinate negation: a real negation has content, so you can point at it. Vague unease is not a
defect.

*Treats:* over-correction. In measured multi-turn self-review, roughly 45% of correct answers flipped
to wrong against about 15% the other way.
*Misuse:* entrenchment. If a defect is real but you cannot yet tell which fix is right, say "cannot
determine" rather than defending or silently editing.

## 2. Keep what still passes its checks; cancel only what the named defect contradicts

Two halves of one move: preserve, and stop. A rewrite that discards working parts is not cleaner — it
is a second unverified change stacked on the first.

*Treats:* the rewrite impulse.
*Misuse:* conservatism dressed as rigour. "It already works" is not a defence where it does not pass
the checks the change requires.

## 3. Name the one constraint that currently blocks acceptance

Say what, while unresolved, prevents the work from being accepted, and what would make it stop
blocking. Then work that one.

*Treats:* editing ten places at once; optimizing what is not binding.
*Misuse:* the classification itself can be wrong, and it moves. Revisit it when the blocker clears
instead of treating it as a permanent property.

## 4. Answer each verification question independently, then check the whole

Ask the check question, answer it from the material alone, and only then compare it to your draft.
Reading your own draft and feeling that it looks right is not verification. After changing a part,
re-run the whole check and report what it broke elsewhere: enumerate callers, consumers, downstream
checks.

*Treats:* verification as a rubber stamp; local fixes that break the system. Independent answering is
the mechanism that keeps a check from being anchored to the draft you are checking.
*Misuse:* budget, in both directions. Each independent answer is another turn — verify the
load-bearing claims, not every sentence. And bound the regression sweep by the tests and callers that
actually reach the change, not by everything that might.

## Sources

Kept separate from the instructions so a move is never decorated with a name it cannot support.

| Move | Attribution |
|---|---|
| 1 | Hegel's *determinate negation*, from the text: "when the result is apprehended as determinate negation, a new form has thereby immediately arisen" (*Phenomenology*, Introduction §79); "because the result, the negation, is a determinate negation, it has a content" (*Science of Logic*). |
| 2 | Hegel's *aufheben*, which he gives as two meanings — to preserve, and to cause to cease (*Science of Logic*, Being §184–186). The common third meaning ("elevate") is an addition of English translation and popular reading, not Hegel's. Do not write "sublation"; write the two actions. |
| 3 | The binding-constraint move for the threshold part: Hegel's node-line of measure relations (*Science of Logic* — water, chemical nodes, the musical scale: the interruption of gradualness, the leap). "Quantity into quality" is the Engels-textbook formula, not Hegel's phrase. |
| 4 | Chain-of-Verification, whose load-bearing design choice is independent answering. The whole-system half is general systems theory and cybernetics, **not** a dialectical tradition — no authoritative source connects feedback loops back to dialectics, so cite it as systems science. |
